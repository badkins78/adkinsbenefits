Medicare Supplement Plan G is a standardized Medicare
Supplement insurance policy designed to help pay many
of the out-of-pocket expenses associated with Original
Medicare.

Medicare Supplement insurance is also known as Medigap
because it helps fill certain coverage gaps left by
Medicare Part A and Part B.

Plan G is one of the most comprehensive standardized
Medigap plans available.

If you are unfamiliar with Medicare Supplement insurance,
start with our [Medicare Supplement insurance guide](/medicare-supplement/).

## What Does Medicare Supplement Plan G Cover?

Standard Medicare Supplement Plan G covers:

- Medicare Part A coinsurance and hospital costs for
  up to an additional 365 days after Medicare benefits
  are exhausted.
- Medicare Part B coinsurance payments.
- The first three pints of blood.
- Medicare Part A hospice care coinsurance and copayments.
- Skilled nursing facility care coinsurance.
- The Medicare Part A deductible.
- Medicare Part B excess charges.
- Foreign travel emergency coverage, subject to
  policy limits.

After you satisfy your annual Medicare Part B deductible,
standard Plan G generally pays the remaining Medicare-approved
Part B coinsurance and copayments.

## What Does Medicare Supplement Plan G Not Cover?

Although Plan G provides comprehensive supplemental
coverage, it does not cover every healthcare expense.

Plan G does not cover:

- The Medicare Part B deductible.
- Routine dental care.
- Routine vision care and eyeglasses.
- Hearing aids.
- Most long-terrm custodial care.
- Outpatient prescription drug coverage.

Medicare beneficiaries who want outpatient prescription
drug coverage can generally purchase a separate
[Medicare Part D plan](/partd/).

## What Is the Difference Between Plan G and Plan F?

Standard Plan G and Plan F provide the same standardized
benefits except for one important difference:
Plan F covers the Medicare Part B deductible, while
Plan G does not.

Plan F is generally unavailable to individuals who
first became eligible for Medicare on or after
January 1, 2020.

People who became eligible before that date may still
be able to purchase Plan F, depending on availability
and eligibility requirements.

Even when both plans are available, comparing annual
premiums and expected out-of-pocket costs can help
determine which option offers better value.

## What Is the Difference Between Plan G and Plan N?

Plan N is another standardized Medicare Supplement
option that may offer a lower monthly premium than
Plan G.

Unlike Plan G, Plan N may require copayments of up
to $20 for certain office visits and up to $50 for
emergency room visits that do not result in an
inpatient admission.

Plan N also does not cover Medicare Part B excess
charges.

When comparing Plan G and Plan N, consider the
differenece in premiums, expected doctor visits,
potential copayments, and your preference for
predictable healthcare expenses.

## Can I Choose My Own Doctors With Plan G?

With standard Medicare Supplement Plan G, you generally
have the flexibility to receive care from doctors and
hospitals throughout the United States that accept
Medicare patients.

Unlike many Medicare Advantage plans, standard
Medigap policies generally do not use their own
provider networks.

Medicare SELECT policies are an exception and may
have network requirements.

If provider flexibility is important to you, it is
worth comparing Medicare Supplement coverage with
[Medicare Advantage](/partc/) before making a decision.

## Are All Medicare Supplement Plan G Policies the Same?

The standardized benefits of regular Plan G are
the same regardless of which insurance company
issues the policy.

However, insurance companies can differ in several
important ways:

- Monthly premiums.
- Household discounts.
- Premium rating methods.
- Historical rate increases.
- Customer service.
- Underwriting requirements.

A higher premium does not necessarily provide
better standardized Medicare Supplement benefits.

Comparing multiple insurance companies can help
you identify coverage that fits your budget.

High-deductible Plan G is a separate option with
different cost-sharing requirements.

## When Can I Enroll in Medicare Supplement Plan G?

Your federal Medigap Open Enrollment Period generally
begins the first month you are both age 65 or older
and enrolled in Medicare Part B.

This period lasts six months and provides important
protections when purchasing Medicare Supplement
insurance.

Outside this period, insurance companies may require
medical underwriting unless you qualify for a
guaranteed issue right or another applicable protection.

If you already have Medicare Supplement coverage
and are considering changing insurance companies,
review your eligibility before canceling your
existing policy.
