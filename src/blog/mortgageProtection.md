Buying a home is one of the largest financial
commitments many families make. But what would happen
to your mortgage if your family suddenly lost an income
because of an unexpected death?

Mortgage protection life insurance can help provide
your family with the financial resources needed to
manage that obligation.

## How Does Mortgage Protection Insurance Work?

Mortgage protection generally refers to life insurance
selected with your mortgage and household financial
obligations in mind.

If the insured person dies while the policy is in
force and the claim is payable, the insurance company
pays a death benefit to the designated beneficiary.

Depending on the policy and beneficiary arrangement,
that money may be used to:

- Pay off the remaining mortgage balance.
- Continue making monthly mortgage payments.
- Replace lost household income.
- Pay property taxes and homeowners insurance.
- Cover other debts and household expenses.
- Provide financial support while the family adjusts
  to the loss of an income.

The objective is to give your family financial
flexibility during a difficult time.

## Is Mortgage Protection Insurance the Same as PMI?

No. Private mortgage insurance, commonly called PMI,
is designed to protect the mortgage lender if the
borrower defaults on the loan.

Mortgage protection life insurance is intended to
help protect your family from the financial
consequences of losing an insured person.

Mortgage protection life insurance is generally
optional and does not replace any mortgage insurance
required by your lender.

## How Much Mortgage Protection Insurance Do I Need?

Your remaining mortgage balance is a useful starting
point, but it is not the only consideration.

When determining an appropriate coverage amount,
consider:

- Your remaining mortgage balance.
- Your monthly mortgage payment.
- The number of years remaining on your loan.
- How much income your family would lose.
- Other outstanding debts.
- Existing life insurance coverage.
- Available savings and other financial resources.
- The financial needs of your dependents.

For example, a homeowner with a $250,000 mortgage
might consider $250,000 in life insurance coverage
to help provide enough money to pay off the loan.

However, that same homeowner may need additional
coverage to replace income or meet other household
expenses.

The appropriate amount depends on your family's
overall financial situation and budget.

## What Type of Life Insurance Is Used for Mortgage Protection?

Term life insurance is often considered for mortgage
protection because it provides coverage for a
specified period of time.

A homeowner with 20 years remaining on a mortgage
might consider a 20-year term life insurance policy,
depending on available options and eligibility.

Permanent life insurance may also be appropriate
when the need for coverage extends beyond the
remaining mortgage term.

The right policy depends on your age, health,
financial goals, budget, and how long you need
the coverage to remain in place.

## How Much Does Mortgage Protection Insurance Cost?

There is no single price for mortgage protection
insurance.

Premiums can depend on several factors, including:

- Your age.
- Your health and medical history.
- Tobacco use.
- The amount of coverage requested.
- The type and duration of the policy.
- Available policy features and riders.
- The insurance company's underwriting requirements.

Different insurance companies may evaluate the same
applicant differently.

Comparing available policies can help you understand
your options before committing to coverage.

## Can I Get Mortgage Protection Insurance Without a Medical Exam?

Some life insurance companies offer policies that
do not require a traditional medical examination.

However, an application may still include health
questions, prescription history, medical records,
or other underwriting requirements.

Eligibility, coverage amounts, premiums, and
application requirements vary by insurance company
and policy.

A policy that does not require a medical examination
does not necessarily guarantee approval.

## Does Mortgage Protection Cover Disability or Illness?

A standard life insurance death benefit is not
designed to replace income simply because you
become disabled or unable to work.

Some policies may include or offer accelerated
death benefit riders that allow access to part
of the death benefit following a qualifying
illness or medical condition.

Other insurance products or riders may provide
different types of disability-related protection.

These benefits are not included in every policy.
Eligibility requirements, exclusions, costs,
and benefit limitations should be reviewed
before purchasing coverage.

## Do I Need Mortgage Protection If I Already Have Life Insurance?

Not necessarily.

Your existing life insurance may already provide
enough coverage to help your family manage the
mortgage and other financial obligations.

Before purchasing additional coverage, review
your existing policies, coverage amounts,
expiration dates, and your family's financial needs.

The goal is to identify whether a coverage gap
exists rather than purchase unnecessary insurance.

## Can I Purchase Mortgage Protection After Buying a Home?

You do not necessarily need to purchase mortgage
protection life insurance when you close on
your mortgage.

Homeowners may consider coverage after purchasing
a home, refinancing an existing mortgage, or
experiencing changes in their family's financial
circumstances.

Your eligibility and available options will depend
on the insurance company's requirements at the
time you apply.
